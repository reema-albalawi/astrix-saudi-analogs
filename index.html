The problem

Missions keep failing to the same environments.

Dust, cold, darkness and loose ground have ended or crippled missions for sixty years. Testing on Earth helps only if the test site reproduces the right combination of conditions.

Our question

Can we predict how space systems degrade under repeated environmental stress, and point to the Earth sites where those scenarios can be tested before launch?

How it works

From satellite pixels to a test plan.

STEP 1

Discover

Find under-documented craters, sinkholes and cavities from imagery and literature.

STEP 2

Measure

Diameter, depth and rim slope from NASADEM / SRTM elevation (≈30 m).

STEP 3

Compare

Match to lunar and Martian features using Moon Trek, Mars Trek, LRO Diviner and HiRISE.

STEP 4

Score

Weighted readiness across stability, mobility, thermal, light and shelter.

STEP 5

Recommend

Pair sites and seasons with a system and the failures it must survive.

Limits of the analog

What the desert cannot reproduce.

Being clear about differences makes the results credible.

Gravity

1 g on Earth, 0.17 g on the Moon, 0.38 g on Mars. Soil and wheel behaviour change with it.

Vacuum & atmosphere

No air on the Moon and 0.6 % of Earth's pressure on Mars. Heat moves very differently.

Radiation

Earth's magnetic field and air block most of what reaches lunar and Martian surfaces.

Scale

Al Wahbah is about 2 km wide. Shackleton crater at the lunar south pole is about 21 km.

Data resolution

SRTM's ≈30 m pixels can miss small pits and skylights. Higher-resolution sources will be cited separately.

Dust chemistry

Lunar regolith is sharp and electrostatically charged. Desert sand is rounded by wind.
